## Overview

Signy is a document management and digital signature platform I led from idea to MVP. The product started from a clear market gap: existing tools could handle parts of the job, but very few supported the full transition from document creation, editable forms, routing, signing, tracking, and final legally valid PDF output.

The goal was not to create another signature feature. It was to modernize document-heavy workflows without forcing organizations to abandon the way they already worked.

## The opportunity

The market was fragmented. Some products were good at signatures. Others were good at forms, storage, or sending. What was missing was a connected workflow that preserved the operational reality of teams handling contracts, approvals, and formal documents every day.

That gap shaped the product thesis:

- Take an existing document and turn it into a structured digital workflow.
- Let teams assign fields, configure recipients, and define the signing order.
- Support multi-channel delivery and full tracking from send to completion.
- End with an output that was still legally usable and operationally practical.

## Validation before build

Before development, I ran market research and competitor analysis across document management, digital forms, and signing products. The conclusion was consistent: the pain was not one missing feature, but the number of disconnected steps teams had to manage.

I then interviewed 10 potential customers. Four agreed to enter a pilot and showed real willingness to pay if the product solved the workflow in a practical way. That validation mattered because it meant we were not building from intuition alone.

One early real-world use case came from the Jerusalem Symphony Orchestra, which needed a better way to manage contracts and remote signing across distributed participants.

## What I owned

I led the project end to end as Product Manager, Project Manager, and System Analyst. That included:

- Defining the product scope, roadmap, and decision logic.
- Translating product goals into requirements, edge cases, and release criteria.
- Leading a 6-person cross-functional team across front-end, back-end, design, and QA.
- Running sprint priorities, coordination, and quality control.
- Supporting implementation decisions across workflow logic, integrations, and delivery blockers.

This was not just delivery management. I was responsible for what we were building, why it mattered, how it should behave, and how to move the team from idea to shipped product.

## Core product flows

The platform was structured around 3 main product areas: file management, document editing, and the recipient signature experience.

### Sender workflow

- Upload a document or form into the system.
- Place fields directly on the document and turn a static file into an actionable flow.
- Select recipients, define signing order, and configure sending rules.
- Distribute documents through multiple channels such as email, WhatsApp, SMS, and connected messaging flows.
- Track recipient activity from send to completion.
- Deliver the final signed output into connected workflows such as Google Drive.

The sender experience was designed to be practical and fast, with a target setup time of about 3 minutes.

### Signer workflow

The signer experience was optimized for speed and clarity. Recipients could open a link, review the document, complete required fields, and sign from any device with minimal friction. The full flow could be completed in under a minute for simple cases.

The product direction also included multiple identification methods such as Google, Facebook, OTP, and email-based flows. I intentionally preserved a low-friction self-service path so users could try the system on their own and convert into new leads.

## Go-to-market and commercial thinking

I designed a 3-tier pricing model and positioned it as a low-to-mid market entry offer to support a penetration strategy. The pricing was part of the product, not an afterthought. It helped guide leads toward the recommended plan while matching the maturity of the MVP and the way customers were likely to buy.

Within a week, we also launched a marketing website connected to the product engine. I shaped a clean, blue, technology-forward direction and made sure the setup included SEO, analytics, and measurement so we could test messaging, acquisition, and early funnel assumptions.

## Delivery under real constraints

We worked in a Scrum-style rhythm with two-week sprints and clearly defined outputs. I built delivery plans around the roadmap, kept scope aligned to release goals, and stayed involved in the technical and operational detail needed to unblock progress.

The team itself was distributed across time zones and mixed full-time and part-time contributors. That created real coordination complexity. On top of that, some contributors were working under unstable logistical conditions during conflict periods. In one case, I even bought a generator for a developer so work could continue reliably. That detail captures how I approached leadership on the project: I took responsibility not only for the roadmap, but for the team’s ability to keep shipping.

## Technical depth

This was also the project where my systems thinking became much more practical. I went deeper into platform logic, data structure decisions, integrations, troubleshooting, and technical tradeoffs than I had before.

Core stack:

- Front-end: Nuxt.js and Vue
- Back-end: Node.js and NestJS
- Database: MySQL
- Integrations: Google OAuth 2.0, Firebase, Telegram, WhatsApp, SMS
- Infrastructure and tools: GitHub, Jira, Figma, Nginx, Ubuntu Server, Docker, Nx, IBM digital signature library

That proximity to implementation strengthened the way I now think about product architecture and cross-functional execution.

## Outcome

Signy resulted in a differentiated MVP for document management and digital signature workflows. It validated a real market need, created pilot traction, and established a foundation for pricing, onboarding, and future product growth.

For me personally, it was one of the most formative projects in my career. It sharpened my product judgment, strengthened my technical depth, and reinforced the kind of work I do best: identifying opportunity, validating before overbuilding, organizing people around a clear direction, and carrying a complex initiative from concept to implementation.
